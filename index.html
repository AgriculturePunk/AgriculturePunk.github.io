Manifesto

A scarecrow staked into a rice field and an LED plugged into a breadboard are the same gesture.

In Japanese agriculture, scarecrows have long been staked into fields as vessels for the paddy deity — welcomed during the growing season, ritually pulled after harvest to release the deity back to the mountains. In electronics prototyping, components are plugged and unplugged on breadboards to compose circuits. Deity and electricity are operationally equivalent: both invisible, both inhabiting a site through object placement, both governed by plugging and unplugging.

Agriculture Punk is a movement toward the computerization of farmland — not by covering fields with sensors, but by rereading what farmers already do: observing, judging, believing, and performing ritual, as acts of connection and disconnection. The field is a substrate. The tools are components. The ritual is the program.

Hello, world — from the field.

Farmland = Breadboard

  • Scarecrow inserted into the farmland = Component inserted into the breadboard
  • Farmland as a grid = Breadboard as a grid
  • Paddy deity inhabits the site through placement = Electricity inhabits the site through placement
  • Staking, tying, circling, pulling = Wiring, connecting, routing, unplugging
